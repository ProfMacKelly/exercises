### Practice hypotheticals

**Affirmative Defenses**
1. A car dealer sells a used car to a 17-year-old who signs a financing contract alone. Two weeks later, the teen returns the car and tries to unwind the contract. The dealer argues “a deal is a deal.” The minor shrugs their shoulders, doesn’t make any payments, and is sued for breach of contract. 
    

2. A home-health aide tells an isolated patient, “If you don’t sign your cabin over to me today, I’ll stop visiting.” The patient signs. Later, the patient refuses to submit the government documentation that officially transfers ownership. The health aide sues for breach of contract. 
    

3. A shipping company refuses to release a customer’s perishable inventory unless the customer signs a new contract doubling the storage rate “immediately.” The customer signs to avoid spoilage, and later refuses to pay the increased rate. The company sues for breach of contract 
    

4. A payday lender hands a borrower a “one-page” agreement. The back page includes a 400% interest term and a clause requiring arbitration in another state. The borrower signs without seeing it. The borrower wants to get out of the contract but the lender refuses. 
    

5. A restaurant signs a one-year lease for “Suite 210,” believing it includes a working kitchen hood. The landlord also believes the hood is permitted and operational. After signing, both learn the hood was condemned and cannot be used. 
    

6. A contractor bids $15,000 for a job, but a spreadsheet error dropped a zero (it should have been $150,000). The owner accepts immediately. Once the contractor discovers the error, they refuse to start work. The owner sues for breach of contract and asks the court for specific performance (i.e., ordering the contractor to perform the job agreed upon for the $15,000 agreed upon). 
    

7. A seller tells a buyer, “The roof was replaced last year,” and shows an invoice that turns out to be fake. The buyer signs and later discovers the roof is 20 years old and leaking. The buyer has to replace to roof due to leakage and sues the seller. The buyer claims there was a contract, so they shouldn’t have to pay for a new roof. 
    

8. A manufacturer agrees to deliver 1,000 custom parts at a fixed price. Before production, a new government regulation bans the only legally compliant material for those parts, and no substitute exists. The manufacturer stops performance and the customer sues for breach of contract. 
    

9. A buyer and seller orally agree to sell a vacant lot for $80,000. The buyer pays a $5,000 deposit, but nothing is signed. The seller later backs out and sells to someone else. The buyer sues for breach of contract.
